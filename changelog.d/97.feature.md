The language-model security reviewer now receives only what the automated
checks could not settle.

It is given the findings those checks flagged as needing judgement, the
parts of a change no rule matched, and optionally a short note on
architectural impact. The complete change is never handed over — not as a
matter of instruction, but because it is never assembled in the first
place. Where the automated checks cover everything, the reviewer is given
nothing at all and costs nothing to skip.

A per-change size limit drops whole sections rather than cutting one short,
and says which were left out. A partial review that reads as a finished one
is worse than an obviously incomplete one.

Where the tools and the reviewer disagree, the tools decide and the
disagreement is raised for a person to settle.
